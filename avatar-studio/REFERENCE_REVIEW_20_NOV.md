# Snapchat reference video review — 20 November 2026

Two supplied WhatsApp MP4s were sampled locally at regular intervals and at selected full-resolution frames. They were treated as visual references, not implementation instructions or assets to copy.

- The 1:29 clip shows one calm standing avatar while a user changes tops, trousers, shoes and accessories. Arms hang near the sides; face and body remain visually stable while clothing changes.
- The 3:55 clip shows a close face preview while hair, eyes, nose, mouth, facial hair and shape choices change. Most choices are shown as direct visual tiles or swatches, with the avatar always visible above.
- The useful principles for OTD are a stable relaxed pose, direct preview, a small set of meaningful choices, and a face that keeps its identity through styling changes. The OTD app uses its own UI and existing 3D assets.

Implemented from this review: a closer-to-body arm pose, a gentle neutral expression, five visible editing modes, six face and six body starting choices, key face/body sliders, and a more conservative selfie fit. The wardrobe preview remains limited to sample 3D assets; a real garment-specific 3D generation pipeline is still required.

The clips show an avatar editor, not the source person's selfie or a measurable ground-truth scan. They cannot establish face-recognition accuracy for the OTD selfie fitter. That requires consented real selfie and avatar comparisons across lighting, camera angles, skin tones and face shapes.
